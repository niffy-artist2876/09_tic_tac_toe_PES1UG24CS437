● Here are some ideas that are practical in pygame. I've put the biggest visual
  gains first.

  Pick an overall look

  1. Dark modern (my recommendation)
  - A deep navy background (#1E1E2E), with the board on a slightly lighter
    rounded card (#2A2A3C).
  - X in coral (#FF6B6B) and O in teal (#4ECDC4): bright, and easy to tell
    apart.
  - Thin, muted grid lines (#3A3A50) with rounded ends, so the X's and O's stand
    out more than the grid.

  2. Soft pastel / paper
  - A cream background (#FAF7F0), with thick rounded X's and O's in muted
    terracotta and sage green.
  - It feels friendly and hand-made. It's also the closest to the current light
    theme, so it's the smallest change.

  3. Neon arcade
  - A black background with glowing pink and cyan marks. The glow comes from
    drawing each mark a few times, wider and more transparent each time,
    underneath the main stroke.
  - It's the most eye-catching, but it's the most work and can look busy.

  Changes that help whichever look you pick

  1. Center everything. The board is currently stuck to the left edge. Centering
     it, with even margins, is the cheapest big improvement.
  2. Scoreboard as three "cards" in a row along the top: X 2 · Draws 1 · O 0,
     each tinted with its player's color. Show the round result in a pill-shaped
     badge instead of red text.
  3. Highlight the winning line. Draw a thick stroke through the three winning
     cells, which makes diagonal wins obvious. That would have answered your "O
     wins?" question at a glance.
  4. Hover preview. Show a faint X on the empty cell under your mouse. It makes
     the game feel responsive and shows which cells you can click.
  5. Animations.
     - New marks grow from 0 to full size over about 150 ms.
     - The X strokes draw in one after the other.
     - The computer waits about 300 ms before moving, so it feels like a turn.
       At the moment, its move appears in the same frame as yours.
  6. Better font. Consolas looks like a code editor. pygame can load any .ttf
     file, for example a free font like Poppins or Nunito, saved in the project
     folder. That avoids depending on fonts the computer may not have.
  7. Sharp text when resized. Redraw the layout at the actual window size
     instead of scaling a 400×590 image. This fixes the blur I mentioned
     earlier. It's the biggest refactor on this list, since every position would
     have to scale with the window.

  My recommendation

  Go with Dark modern, plus items 1 to 5. That combination looks the most modern
  for the effort and stays well within what pygame does well. Items 6 and 7 are
  extras for later.
